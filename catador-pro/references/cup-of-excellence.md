# Cup of Excellence Protocol — Detailed Reference

## Overview

Cup of Excellence (CoE) is an international competition program run by the Alliance for Coffee Excellence (ACE). It identifies and auctions the best coffees from a participating country each year. The cupping protocol is more rigorous than standard SCA cupping, with higher quality thresholds and a multi-round judging format.

## Competition Format

1. **Pre-selection**: National judges evaluate submitted samples. Coffees scoring 85+ advance.
2. **National Jury**: Expanded national panel re-cups. Top scorers advance.
3. **International Jury**: 20-25 international judges (head judges, Q Graders) cup over 5-7 days.
4. **Final round**: Top coffees are re-cupped to determine rankings.
5. **Auction**: Winning coffees are sold via online auction to the highest bidder.

Coffees must score **86.00+** from the international jury to earn the Cup of Excellence designation.
Coffees scoring **90.00+** earn the **Presidential Award**.

## Scored Attributes (6.00–10.00, 0.25 increments)

The CoE form evaluates 8 scored attributes:

### 1. Clean Cup
Unlike SCA where this is cup-level (2 pts per cup), CoE scores Clean Cup as a quality attribute on the 6-10 scale. Evaluates transparency and freedom from off-flavors across all cups.

### 2. Sweetness
Also scored as a quality attribute (not cup-level). Evaluates the intensity and quality of inherent sweetness. This gives sweetness more weight in the final score compared to SCA format.

### 3. Acidity
Quality and character of acidity. Same as SCA — evaluate both intensity and quality. In CoE context, interesting and distinctive acidity is highly valued.

### 4. Mouthfeel
Similar to SCA Body but with emphasis on tactile quality — weight, texture, and overall mouthfeel experience.

### 5. Flavor
Principal gustatory character. The core of the evaluation.

### 6. Aftertaste
Quality and length of finish. CoE judges tend to weight this heavily — great coffees have long, evolving aftertastes.

### 7. Balance
Harmony of all components.

### 8. Overall
Holistic impression. In CoE, this is where judges express their overall excitement about the coffee. A "wow factor" score.

## Defects

Same classification as SCA:
- Taint: cups × 2
- Fault: cups × 4

Defective coffees are typically eliminated in pre-selection and don't reach the international jury.

## Final Score Calculation

```
Clean Cup:    ___
Sweetness:    ___
Acidity:      ___
Mouthfeel:    ___
Flavor:       ___
Aftertaste:   ___
Balance:      ___
Overall:      ___
──────────────────
Subtotal:     ___
− Defects:    ___
══════════════════
FINAL SCORE:  ___
```

Maximum possible: 80 (8 attributes × 10) minus defects. Wait — that only gives 80 max, not 100. The CoE form adds **+20 base points** to bring the scale to 100:

**FINAL SCORE = 20 + Sum of 8 attributes − Defects**

This means:
- All attributes scored at 7.50 = 20 + 60 = 80 (minimum specialty)
- All attributes scored at 8.25 = 20 + 66 = 86 (CoE qualification)
- All attributes scored at 8.75 = 20 + 70 = 90 (Presidential Award)

## Quality Classification (CoE-specific)

| Score | Classification |
|-------|---------------|
| 90.00+ | Presidential Award — top of competition |
| 86.00–89.99 | Cup of Excellence Award — officially awarded |
| 84.00–85.99 | National Winner — excellent but below CoE threshold |
| < 84 | Does not advance |

## Key Differences from SCA Arabica

| Aspect | SCA Arabica | Cup of Excellence |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| Clean Cup | Cup-level (2pts × 5 cups) | Quality attribute (6-10 scale) |
| Sweetness | Cup-level (2pts × 5 cups) | Quality attribute (6-10 scale) |
| Uniformity | Cup-level (2pts × 5 cups) | Not separately scored |
| Body/Mouthfeel | Body | Mouthfeel |
| Base points | None | +20 |
| Total attributes | 10 | 8 |
| Minimum specialty | 80 | 86 for CoE award |
| Context | Professional evaluation | Competition ranking |

## Judging Calibration

CoE international judges go through calibration sessions before scoring begins. This is crucial because judges come from different countries and cupping traditions. The head judge leads calibration cuppings to align the panel on scoring standards.

When simulating CoE scoring, note that scores tend to be stricter than casual SCA cupping — judges are comparing exceptional coffees against each other, not against the general market.
