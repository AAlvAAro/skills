# SCA Fine Robusta Cupping Protocol — Detailed Reference

## Overview

The Fine Robusta protocol is adapted from the SCA Arabica form but modified to evaluate robusta's unique sensory characteristics fairly. Robusta is not "worse arabica" — it has its own flavor space, and this protocol is designed to assess quality within that space.

## Key Differences from Arabica Protocol

1. **Salt/Acid** replaces Acidity — evaluates the balance between salty and acidic tastes
2. **Bitter/Sweet** replaces Sweetness (cup-level) — evaluates the balance between bitter and sweet
3. **Mouthfeel** replaces Body — broader evaluation of tactile sensations
4. Same scoring range (6.00–10.00, 0.25 increments)
5. Same 100-point calculation

## Cupping Preparation

Same as SCA Arabica:
- 8.25g coffee per 150ml water
- Water at 200°F (93°C)
- 5 cups per sample
- Light sample roast

## Scored Attributes (6.00–10.00, 0.25 increments)

### Fragrance/Aroma
Same evaluation as Arabica: dry, wet (crust), break. Robusta aromatics often include:
- Earthy, woody, grain, cereal
- Dark chocolate, cocoa
- Spice (pepper, clove)
- Nutty (peanut, hazelnut)
- Fine robustas: floral, fruity notes

### Flavor
Principal character of the coffee. Fine robustas can show surprising complexity — dark chocolate, spice, dried fruit, nuts. Low-quality robusta tends toward rubbery, harsh, or flat.

### Aftertaste
Length and quality of lingering flavor. Fine robustas often have a pleasant chocolatey or nutty finish.

### Salt/Acid
This is the key distinguishing attribute. Evaluate the balance:
- **High quality**: Acidity present and pleasant, salty taste minimal or absent
- **Moderate quality**: Some saltiness, moderate acidity
- **Low quality**: Dominant salty taste, harsh or absent acidity

Mark intensity (high/medium/low) and score quality.

### Bitter/Sweet
Evaluate the balance between bitterness and sweetness:
- **High quality**: Sweetness dominant, bitterness is clean and pleasant (like dark chocolate)
- **Moderate quality**: Balance between bitter and sweet
- **Low quality**: Harsh, astringent bitterness with no sweetness

### Mouthfeel
Broader than Arabica's "Body" — evaluates all tactile sensations:
- Weight and thickness
- Smoothness vs. astringency
- Creaminess
- Overall tactile quality

### Balance
How well all attributes work together. Same concept as Arabica.

### Overall
Personal holistic impression.

## Cup-Level Attributes

### Uniformity (2 pts per cup, max 10)
Same as Arabica — consistency across all 5 cups.

### Clean Cup (2 pts per cup, max 10)
Same as Arabica — absence of non-coffee flavors.

## Defects

Same classification as Arabica:
- Taint: cups × 2
- Fault: cups × 4

Common robusta defects: rubber, harsh bitterness, medicinal, ferment, earthy (in excess).

## Final Score Calculation

Same formula as Arabica:
```
Sum of all attributes (Fragrance/Aroma + Flavor + Aftertaste + Salt/Acid + Bitter/Sweet + Mouthfeel + Balance + Uniformity + Clean Cup + Overall) − Defects = Final Score
```

## Quality Classification

| Score | Classification |
|-------|---------------|
| 90–100 | Outstanding Fine Robusta |
| 85–89.99 | Excellent Fine Robusta |
| 80–84.99 | Very Good Fine Robusta |
| < 80 | Below Fine Robusta Grade |

## Context for Analysis

When analyzing a Fine Robusta cupping, consider:
- **Origin**: Vietnam, Indonesia, Uganda, India, and Brazil are major producers
- **Altitude**: Robusta grows at lower altitudes (0-800 masl typically)
- **Processing**: Natural process is most common; washed robusta is less common but can be distinctive
- **Blending**: Many robustas are evaluated for espresso blend potential — body and crema contribution
