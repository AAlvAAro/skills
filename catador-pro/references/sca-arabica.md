# SCA Arabica Cupping Protocol — Detailed Reference

## Cupping Preparation

- **Roast**: Light sample roast, Agtron 58-63 (whole bean), 24-48 hours rest after roasting
- **Grind**: Medium-coarse, ground immediately before cupping (within 15 min)
- **Ratio**: 8.25g coffee per 150ml water
- **Water**: 200°F (93°C), clean, odor-free, 125-175 ppm TDS
- **Cups**: 5 cups per sample, minimum

## Evaluation Sequence

1. **Fragrance** (dry) — Evaluate ground coffee aroma before water contact
2. **Aroma** (wet) — Evaluate after adding water, during the break (3-5 min steep)
3. **Break** — At 3-5 minutes, break the crust with a spoon, evaluate aroma released
4. **Skim** — Remove grounds from surface
5. **Taste** (8-10 min, when cooled to ~70°C) — Evaluate: Flavor, Aftertaste, Acidity, Body, Balance
6. **Cool-down** — Continue evaluating as coffee cools to room temperature — flavors change
7. **Sweetness, Clean Cup, Uniformity** — Evaluate across all 5 cups
8. **Overall** — Your personal assessment of the total experience
9. **Defects** — Note any defective cups

## Attribute Scoring Guide

All scored attributes use a 6.00-10.00 scale in 0.25 increments.

### Fragrance/Aroma
Evaluate in three phases:
1. **Dry fragrance**: Sniff the dry grounds (rate intensity and quality)
2. **Wet aroma (crust)**: After adding water, before breaking
3. **Break aroma**: During the break at 3-5 minutes (most revealing)

Record a single combined score. Note specific aromas detected.

### Flavor
The most important single attribute. Represents the coffee's principal character — the combined impression of all gustatory (taste bud) and retronasal (aroma via palate) sensations. Evaluate mid-palate: what do you taste when the coffee first hits?

### Aftertaste
The length, quality, and pleasantness of flavor that lingers after swallowing/expectorating. Positive aftertaste = higher score. Short or unpleasant aftertaste = lower score.

### Acidity
Evaluate two dimensions:
- **Intensity**: How pronounced is the acidity? (mark on the scale: high-medium-low)
- **Quality**: Is it bright and lively, or sour and sharp?

High-quality acidity is valued — it brings life and complexity. A score of 8+ means the acidity is vibrant, complex, and enhances the coffee. Low-quality acidity (sharp, sour, vinegary) scores lower even if intensity is high.

### Body
Evaluate two dimensions:
- **Level**: How heavy/thick does the coffee feel? (mark: full-medium-light)
- **Quality**: Is it pleasant and tactile, or thin and papery?

A light-bodied coffee can score high if the body is silky, clean, and appropriate for the coffee. A heavy-bodied coffee can score low if it's muddy or astringent.

### Balance
How well do flavor, aftertaste, acidity, and body work together? A balanced coffee has all elements in harmony — none overshadowing the others. An unbalanced coffee may have excellent acidity but thin body, or great body but flat flavor.

### Uniformity
Score 2 points per cup (max 10). All 5 cups should taste identical. If any cup differs noticeably from the others, deduct 2 points for that cup.

### Clean Cup
Score 2 points per cup (max 10). Absence of non-coffee tastes or aromas from ingestion to aftertaste. If any cup has off-flavors, deduct 2 points for that cup.

### Sweetness
Score 2 points per cup (max 10). Presence of inherent, pleasant sweetness. Most specialty coffees score full marks. Deduct 2 points for any cup lacking sweetness.

### Overall
Your personal holistic impression. This is where the cupper's individual experience matters. A coffee that technically scores well on individual attributes but doesn't "sing" together might get a lower Overall. Conversely, a coffee with modest individual scores but an extraordinary gestalt might get a higher Overall.

### Defects
Identify any defective cups and classify:

| Type | Intensity | Points per cup |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Taint | Slight off-flavor, noticeable but not overwhelming | 2 |
| Fault | Definite off-flavor, makes the cup undrinkable | 4 |

**Total Defect Score** = Number of cups affected × Intensity points

Common defects: ferment, phenol/chemical, Rio, rubber, stinker, earthy, musty, baggy, potato (East African), past crop.

## Quality Classification

| Score | Classification | Meaning |
|-------|---------------|---------|
| 90.00–100 | Outstanding | Top of class, extraordinary complexity and character |
| 85.00–89.99 | Excellent | Distinctive origin character, complex, very clean |
| 80.00–84.99 | Very Good | Specialty grade, solid quality, clear positive attributes |
| 75.00–79.99 | Good | Not quite specialty, some positive but also some negatives |
| 70.00–74.99 | Fair | Commercial grade, basic |
| < 70 | Poor | Significant defects |

## Practical Tips for Accurate Scoring

- **Calibrate first**: If cupping with others, cup a reference coffee together to align scores
- **Score as you go**: Don't wait until the end to assign scores — score each attribute as you evaluate it
- **Revisit as it cools**: Flavors evolve significantly as coffee cools. Some coffees improve dramatically; others fall apart
- **Score the quality, not your preference**: You might not personally love a very acidic Kenya, but if the acidity is high quality, score it high
- **Defects are not the same as low quality**: A clean coffee can still score 78 if the positive attributes are modest. Defects are specific off-flavors, not just "meh"
